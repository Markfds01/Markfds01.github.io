Teaching Assistant, Computational Physics

I have the pleasure of assisting with the practical sessions of Computational Physics, a second-year Physics BSc course run by Alberto Aleta — the part where students write the methods themselves, in C: Euler and Verlet, Monte Carlo, molecular dynamics, the Ising model, simulated annealing, networks.

A couple of the projects we have built for it, each a real program the students write over the term and then get ambushed with an extension in the exam:

Project · 2025 / 2026

Stochastic Parrots

Implement Llama 2 inference in C from scratch, then run a real language model on a laptop. The point of the project is that none of it is beyond a first-year course: the entire forward pass is matrix multiplication, a normalisation, and drawing a sample from a distribution. Students write every one of those themselves, importing nothing — and find that the systems everyone talks about as though they were opaque are built from the maths they already have.

The students found that the parrots have opinions about whether they will pass the exam, so in the exam we asked them to put the question to the model. Apparently none had tried beforehand, and the parrots were not optimistic.

Project · 2025 / 2026

Doomscrolling

A student wanders the first floor of the Faculty of Science the week before exams, trying to ambush professors with last-minute questions — but with their eyes on TikTok, so they move by a correlated random walk. Stress accumulates, finding a professor resets it, and running out of time triggers a panicked flight from the building. Stochastic movement, von Mises headings, collision detection and real-time physics in SDL3.

For the exam, on the anniversary of the 2025 Iberian Peninsula blackout, we switched the lights off: the student could see only what fell inside the cone lit by their phone, and had to find the professors in the dark while the battery drained.
