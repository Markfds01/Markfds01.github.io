Teaching Assistant, Computational Physics

I have the pleasure of assisting with the practical sessions of Computational Physics, a second-year Physics BSc course run by Alberto Aleta — the part where students write the methods themselves, in C: Euler and Verlet, Monte Carlo, molecular dynamics, the Ising model, simulated annealing, networks.

A couple of the projects we have built for it, each a real program the students write over the term and then get ambushed with an extension in the exam:

Project · 2025 / 2026

Stochastic Parrots

Implement Llama 2 inference in C from scratch and then run a small LLM on a regular laptop. Nothing in it is beyond a first-year course: the forward pass is matrix multiplication, a normalisation, and drawing from a distribution.

In the exam we had them ask the model whether they would pass. Apparently nobody had thought to try it first, and the parrots were not optimistic.

Project · 2025 / 2026

Doomscrolling

A student hunts professors on the first floor the week before exams, eyes on TikTok, moving by a correlated random walk. Stress accumulates, finding a professor resets it, running out of time triggers a panicked flight.

For the exam, on the anniversary of the 2025 Iberian blackout, we switched the lights off: only the cone lit by the phone is visible, and the battery drains.

The assignment (left) and the exam blackout extension (right). Stress and battery tick down; finding a professor buys back some calm. Recordings by Alberto Aleta.
